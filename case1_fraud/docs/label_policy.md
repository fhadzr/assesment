# Case 1 Label Policy

## The two observed labels and their semantics

| Label | Generation | Prevalence (defaults) | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `blocked` | Noisy operational proxy: catches ~80% of ground-truth fraud (20% miss rate) and false-flags part of the high-amount legitimate tail (top-2% amounts, 50% flagged) | ~2.7% | **Training target** |
| `confirmed_fraud` | Ground-truth fraud thinned by `--confirmed-rate` (default 0.5) | ~1% | **Primary evaluation label** |

`blocked` models reality: operational blocking systems over- and under-shoot.
`confirmed_fraud` models adjudication: investigation outcomes are trusted but
only a fraction of fraud is ever investigated.

## Guarantees

1. **No conflation.** The columns are generated by independent processes
   (generate.py) and consumed by distinct pipeline stages (train.py reads
   `blocked`; evaluate.py reports primarily against `confirmed_fraud`).
2. **No fabrication.** `confirmed_fraud ⊆ ground_truth_fraud`, always
   (asserted by tests/test_labels.py::test_confirmed_is_subset_of_ground_truth_never_fabricated).
3. **No conversion.** No code path writes `confirmed_fraud`; evaluation loads
   it read-only and asserts cell-for-cell equality with the generated table
   (evaluate.py `no_conversion_asserted`, tests/test_evaluation.py).
4. **Noise documented at train time.** model metadata records the blocked-label
   noise assumption (catch rate 0.80, false-positive tail) and cites the
   dataset manifest as its source (tests/test_evaluation.py::test_metadata_records...).

## Why train on the noisier label?

`blocked` is denser and available for every row; `confirmed_fraud` is sparse.
Training on `blocked` uses all rows; evaluating on `confirmed_fraud` measures
generalization to the trusted concept. The gap between the two evaluations
(tests/test_model.py, metrics.json) quantifies proxy-label bias — a feature of
this prototype, not a defect.

| Claim | Test |
| --- | --- |
